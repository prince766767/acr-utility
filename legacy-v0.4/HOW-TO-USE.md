# ACR Utility — how to use it

**One file: `ACR-Utility.html`.** Open it and it works. No installation, no account, no
internet, no server required. Everything you type is saved on the device you typed it on,
immediately, whether or not you ever sign in.

It covers the Himachal Pradesh / UGC PBAS *Annual Performance Based Appraisal* (Appendix-IV):
Part-I and Part-II, points 1 to 30 — your portion. Part-III and Part-IV (points 31–52, the
Principal, the Screening-cum-Evaluation Committee and the Reviewing Officer) are printed
blank, in the official order and wording, cleanly typeset and ready to be completed by hand.

You start from a blank form. Fill in your details once under **Profile & Part-I** and they
carry forward automatically into every new year from then on.

---

## Signing in with Google (recommended)

Open **Years & backup** and press **Sign in with Google**, in the new **Account & cloud
sync** card at the top. Google will ask you to approve one narrow permission — the app can
only create and use its own **"ACR Utility"** folder in your Google Drive; it cannot see,
read, or change anything else anywhere in your Drive, Gmail, or Photos.

Once you're signed in:

- A visible **ACR Utility** folder appears in your own Google Drive, with one file per year.
- Every edit is saved on this device immediately, exactly as before, and then synced to
  Drive automatically in the background a few seconds after you stop typing. There is
  nothing to configure and nothing to remember to press.
- A small status chip shows what's happening: **☁ Synced**, **↻ Syncing…**,
  **⚠ Offline — saved locally**, or **⚠ Sync needs attention** (almost always meaning
  "sign in again").
- Open the utility on another device — a phone, a second computer — and sign in with the
  *same* Google account: your complete year archive appears automatically. No import needed.
- If two devices are both edited offline before reconnecting, neither edit is ever silently
  lost — you'll see a clearly labelled **"— Conflict Copy"** record to review instead.

Export Backup, Import Backup, and everything described below continue to work exactly the
same, signed in or not — they're your emergency recovery path if Drive or your Google
account is ever unavailable.

## Opening it

### Windows

Double-click `ACR-Utility.html`. It opens in Edge or Chrome.

Keep the file somewhere permanent — Documents, not Downloads — and consider right-clicking →
*Pin to Start* or making a desktop shortcut.

### Android

1. Put the file in your phone's storage (Drive, WhatsApp to yourself, or a USB cable — anything).
2. Open **Files** → tap `ACR-Utility.html` → **Open with Chrome**.
3. In Chrome: **⋮ → Add to Home screen**. After that it opens with one tap like an app.

### iPhone / iPad

1. Save the file into the **Files** app (iCloud Drive or On My iPhone).
2. Tap it — Safari opens it.
3. Tap the **share button → Add to Home Screen** for one-tap access.

> On iPhone, the file must be opened from the Files app, not from an email attachment
> preview, or Safari may not be able to save your work between sessions.

### If you would rather have a web address

Upload `ACR-Utility.html` to any static host (GitHub Pages, Netlify Drop, your college server)
and open the link. It behaves identically and installs as a proper app on all three platforms.
This is also the easiest way to use the same copy on several devices.

## Working on it over weeks

The ACR is not filled in a day, so the utility is built around stopping and coming back.

- **It saves as you type.** The indicator next to the year selector shows *Saving…* then
  *Saved · 14:32*. There is no Save button to forget — though `Ctrl+S` forces one.
- **Close anything you like.** Tab, browser, laptop lid, phone. Reopen the file and you are
  exactly where you left off, on the section you were last editing.
- **Work in any order.** Use the list on the left (a drop-down on a phone) to jump between
  parts, or the **Previous / Next** buttons at the foot of every screen to walk straight
  through the form from beginning to end. On a keyboard, `Alt`+`→` and `Alt`+`←` do the same.
  Nothing needs to be finished before anything else.

The sections map onto the form:

| Section in the app | Points on the form |
|---|---|
| Profile & Part-I | Cover details and points 1–16 |
| Points 17–19 | Contribution, extra work, time table, assignments, activities, books read, teaching problems |
| Point 20 | Last year's examination results |
| Points 21–25 | New qualifications, courses attended, research work, awards, satisfaction, other points |
| 26 · Category-I | Teaching, learning and evaluation (max 125) |
| 27 · Category-II | Co-curricular, extension, professional development (max 25) |
| 28 · Category-III | Research, publications and academic contributions |
| 29 · Summary | The API summary table |
| 30 · Part-B & enclosures | Other relevant information and the enclosure list |
| Years & backup | Multiple years, backup, transfer between devices |

Tables grow as you need them: **+ Add class**, **+ Add paper**, and so on. The ✕ at the end of
a row removes it.

## How answers are printed

Everything you fill in — free text, table entries, scores, the date-of-birth boxes — is printed
in a colour of your choosing, so an answer never reads as part of the form's own wording. The
default is a dark blue, matching your submitted 2024‑25 ACR.

At the foot of **Profile & Part‑I** you will find *How answers are printed*: five presets
(blue, dark green, dark red, indigo, black), a picker for any other colour, and a
normal / semi‑bold choice. A sample line underneath shows the effect immediately. The setting
applies to the preview, the PDF and the Word file alike, and carries over when you start a new
year.

Choose **black** if your college insists on an all‑black submission — the answers then print in
the same ink as the form.

## Getting the finished ACR out

Three buttons, top right (bottom right on a phone).

### Preview

Shows the finished ACR page by page, exactly as it will print — your Part-I and Part-II filled
in, Part-III and Part-IV following on blank. Read it through before you print anything.

### Word (.docx)

Downloads a real, editable Word document — `ACR_2024-25_PRINCE_THAKUR.docx`. It opens in Word,
Google Docs, LibreOffice, WPS and the Word apps on Android and iPhone. It carries a proper
running footer with page numbers.

Use this when you want to adjust wording by hand, or when the college asks for an editable copy.

### PDF

Opens Preview and then your device's own print dialog.

- **Windows** — choose *Microsoft Print to PDF* or *Save as PDF* as the printer.
- **Android** — in the print sheet, pick *Save as PDF* at the top.
- **iPhone / iPad** — the print preview appears; pinch outwards on the page thumbnail, then use
  the share button to save or send the PDF.

Set **paper size A4** and **margins: Default**, and turn **off** “Headers and footers” in the
browser's print options — the form prints its own footer line.

> If your device's print dialog is awkward, the reliable alternative is: download the Word
> file, open it, and use *File → Save as PDF* / *Export*. The result is identical.

## More than one year

Open **Years & backup**.

- **Start next year from this** takes an existing year and creates the next one: your name,
  father's name, employee code, subject, date of appointment, addresses, college and Principal
  all carry over, and last year's API totals are dropped into the *Last Academic Year* column
  of the summary automatically. Everything year-specific starts empty.
- The drop-down at the top of the screen switches between years at any time.
- **Delete** removes a year permanently from this device.

## Advanced: keeping two computers in step without a Google account

Most teachers should use **Sign in with Google** above instead — it covers phones as well as
computers, with nothing to set up by hand. This section is for anyone who would rather not
use a Google account.

**Your work does not travel by itself.** What you type on the phone lives in the phone's
browser; what you type on the PC lives in the PC's browser. They are separate stores and
nothing bridges them unless you set this up.

For **two computers** (office and home, say), you can make it automatic. Under
**Years & backup → Keep two computers in step**:

1. On the first computer, press **Create a sync file…** and save it *inside your Google Drive
   or OneDrive folder* — the one the desktop app keeps synced to the cloud. Name it something
   like `ACR-sync.acrbak.json`.
2. Wait for Drive to finish uploading.
3. On the second computer, press **Link an existing one…** and pick *that same file*.

From then on, every save is written into the file a few seconds after you stop typing, and
every time you open the utility it reads the file back first. A small **synced** chip appears
next to the year selector.

**How disagreements are settled.** Each year is compared separately, and the copy edited most
recently wins. A computer you have only just set up cannot overwrite real work with its empty
starting copy, whatever its clock says. What it cannot do is merge two people editing the same
year at the same moment — the later save wins outright — so finish on one machine before
starting on the other, and give Drive a moment to upload in between.

**Deleting.** Removing a year deletes it on that computer only; it will return at the next sync
if the file still contains it. Delete it, then press **Sync now** before opening the other
machine.

**Chrome or Edge only**, and computers only. Neither Android nor iPhone gives a browser a real
folder to write into, so phones keep using the export/import route below. If the button is
missing, your browser does not support it.

## Backup and moving between devices

This is the route for phones, and the one worth using regardless as a safety copy.
Under **Years & backup**.

- **Export backup (.acrbak.json)** writes one file containing every year.
- **Import a backup** reads it back. Import adds and updates; it never deletes years already
  on the device.

To move from phone to computer: export on the phone, send the file to yourself (Drive, email,
WhatsApp), open the utility on the computer and import it. And the reverse.

**Do this after any long working session.** Your data lives inside the browser's storage for
this file — on Windows Chrome, buried under
`%LocalAppData%\Google\Chrome\User Data\Default\Local Storage`; on a phone, inside the
browser's private container. You never see it as a file, and clearing “browsing data” or
“site data” will erase it. A backup file in Drive costs nothing and removes the risk entirely.

One reassurance: the data is tied to the *browser*, not to the file. On the same computer you
can move, rename or re-download `ACR-Utility.html` and everything is still there.

## About the API scores

The utility computes a **self-assessment** from the UGC 2010 Appendix-III, Table-I schedule as
reproduced in the form itself. Every figure it produces stays editable — the competent
committee verifies the final score, and where your college reads a rule differently, your
number wins.

What it applies automatically:

- **Category-I** — sub-maxima 50 / 10 / 20 / 20 / 25, total capped at 125 (minimum 75).
  For *(a) Classes taken*, leaving the box blank applies the proportionate rule from the form:
  full 50 at 100 % of assigned classes, proportionate down to 80 %, nothing below 80 %.
- **Category-II** — sub-maxima 20 / 15 / 15, annual total capped at 25 (minimum 15).
- **Category-III** — per-item rates: refereed paper 15, recognised journal 10, conference
  proceeding 10, international chapter 10, national chapter 5, books 50/25/15 (sole) and
  10/5/3 (chapter), projects 20/15/10, consultancy 10 per slab, completed project 20/10,
  outcome 50/30, guidance 10/7/3, training 20/10 (capped at 30 overall), papers presented
  10/7.5/5/3, invited lectures 10/5.
- **Joint work** — the main author receives 60 % and the remaining 40 % is split equally among
  the other authors, as Table-I requires.
- **Science vs Arts** — the project and consultancy money slabs differ between the two streams.
  Set this once in **Profile & Part-I → Subject stream**.

Press **Apply suggested scores** under any Category-III table to fill the API column from these
rules, then adjust individual rows if you need to. The coloured bar at the foot of each
category shows the running total and turns amber if you are below the minimum the form requires.

**Enclosure numbering.** The list of enclosures under *Point 30* is numbered automatically, and
each Category-III row has an *Encl. #* drop-down. Whatever you link there is printed in the
form as `[Encl. 4]` beside the entry, so your evidence is cross-referenced. Attach the physical
enclosures in the order they appear in the list.

## If something goes wrong

**“Storage is blocked” warning, or nothing is being saved.**
You are in a private / incognito window, or the browser is blocking storage for local files.
Open the file in a normal window. If it persists, put the file on a web host instead — storage
always works over http/https. Export a backup before closing the tab either way.

**Work seems to have disappeared.**
Check the year drop-down at the top — you may be looking at a different year. If the browser's
data really was cleared, import your last backup.

**“sync paused — tap to reconnect”.**
Normal. Browsers ask permission again for a linked file after a restart. Tap the chip, or press
**Sync now**, and allow access.

**“sync error — tap to retry”.**
Usually the file is momentarily locked while Drive uploads it, or the folder was moved. Your
work is untouched on the device either way — tap to retry, or link the file again.

**The Word file will not open.**
Make sure it downloaded completely. On Android, open it from the Files app with the Word or
Google Docs app rather than tapping the download notification.

**The printed pages break in an awkward place.**
Long answers naturally push a section onto a second sheet — the official form runs to thirty
pages for the same reason. If a particular break bothers you, download the Word file and adjust
it there.

## What is inside the file

`ACR-Utility.html` is self-contained: the interface, the form definition, the scoring rules,
the Word-document writer and the print layout are all in that one file. Unless you choose to
press **Sign in with Google**, it makes no network requests of any kind. The `src/` folder
and `build.py` are the readable sources it was built from — run `python3 build.py` to rebuild
the single file after editing them.
